## What it is (Contract, from `decision_config.json`, `load_model.py`, `jev_omni.py`)

- **Backbone.** Gemma 4 12B IT. Only the text decoder,
  `Gemma4UnifiedTextModel`, hidden size 3840, is fine-tuned.
  - Two adapters are merged in sequence: "trained v1 rank128" from checkpoint
    root `/out/hard4k-20260919/rank128`, then a LoRA with r = 512,
    α = 512, lr 1e-5.
  - That run is 24,000 examples, 1 epoch, 750 steps × effective batch 32,
    4-GPU DDP, with 2.10B trainable parameters.
  - The card says a "30,000-question fine-tuning run". The config says
    24,000 for the final stage. Unexplained.
- **Media path.** The loader downloads stock `google/gemma-4-12B-it` and
  swaps in the tuned text decoder. The vision and audio towers and the
  processor are the **stock** Gemma 4 ones. Video = 16 frames; audio is capped
  at 30 s via `ffmpeg`.
- **Head.** `Head256`: standardize (`mu` and `sd` buffers), then one linear
  layer 3840 → 256, masked to the first N logits, then softmax.
  - It reads the last-token hidden state of a prompt that **numbers the
    options 1…N** and asks for "only the number".
  - So it classifies **option positions**, not option texts.
  - No temperature or post-hoc calibration appears in the inference path.
    `runtime_buffers.pt` is 19.7 KB and loads into backbone submodules, not
    the head.
- **Confidence** = top probability. This is a third convention: TypeSafe and
  CLM use top minus the mean of the others.
- **Precision gap (Contract).** Their own `verification.json` covers 12
  examples. There, merged vs adapter differs by up to **0.201** in
  probability in the comparison not labelled FP32 (precision unnamed,
  presumably bf16), and by 3.4e-5 in FP32. The public
  multimodal loader `jev_omni.py` casts every decoder `Linear` to **bf16**.
  The card's numbers are "merged-model results" without a precision stated.
  The shipped serving path can therefore shift probabilities by up to about
  0.2 relative to FP32. The ECE claim is unverified for the path users run.
- **Loader risk.** `load_jev_omni()` calls `snapshot_download` on the repo's
  current `main` and `exec_module`s the downloaded `load_model.py`. It is
  remote code by construction. If anyone ever runs it, pin revision
  `c050d513…` and verify `sha256.json`. Under the standing rule, we don't run
  it.

## Label provenance and terms

- **Training data: Unknown.** The 24k (or 30k) training questions and their
  labels are undisclosed.
- **Hypothesis, not established:** the training data comes from the same
  Opus-5 synthetic pipeline as DecisionBench. The `hard4k-20260919`
  checkpoint predates the benchmark by a day. If true, Medium is
  in-distribution for Jev-Omni and out of distribution for Jev. That makes
  trailing Jev there more telling, not less.
- **DecisionBench `results.json`** holds aggregates only: counts, accuracy
  and coverage per model. It has no per-item Jev probabilities, so no Jev
  Output ships in it. Its items are Opus-5-generated, so any training use
  inherits Anthropic's terms on outputs. That status is **Unknown**; check
  the primary terms before any training path.
- **Under plan v3 §3.6** this resolves to `unknown`. Lineage is declared
  (Gemma 4, Apache-2.0), but the training-data parents are missing, and
  missing parents make lineage unknown. It is not `allowed` on its
  self-attestation.

## What it changes for 0.8.0

1. **§3.6 needs a name-vs-lineage test case.**
   - "TypeSafe/Jev, its aliases" must resolve by declared lineage, not by the
     substring "Jev". Matching names do not establish identity.
   - Add a case: a third-party model with "Jev" in its name, a non-Jev
     declared base, a no-Jev-output attestation and undisclosed training
     data. Expected: `unknown` on missing parents, not `barred` by name and
     not `allowed` by attestation.
   - The three derivatives inherit that verdict via `derived_from`.
2. **The multimodal rung's first experiment is the counterfactual this card
   lacks.** Compare the stock backbone with a text-decision-tuned head on
   **media** decisions: same prompt, same options, same metric, with an
   interval. The question is whether decision tuning transfers to media or
   merely coexists with inherited perception. Generic MMAU/MVBench numbers
   can't answer it.
3. **Head design choice: the option-position head (Jev-Omni) vs the
   option-text dual encoder (CLM).**
   - Position heads need a permutation probe first: shuffle options and
     measure how far the probabilities move. This feeds the existing
     position/name-bias diagnosis row.
   - Dual encoders need per-family calibration.
   - Both need ECE with an interval on the serving precision actually
     shipped.
4. **Serving-precision parity is a release check for our own trainer.**
   Evaluate at the precision you ship. Their own file shows 0.2 of drift
   between FP32 and reduced precision.
5. **tabputer-1 feasibility (Hypothesis, untested).**
   - ROCm torch reports a CUDA device, so the loader's check may pass.
   - Size is about 25 GB bf16, or about 50 GB FP32 as shipped; it fits.
   - `transformers==5.17.0` is pinned for `gemma4_unified`.
   - As a recipe to reimplement, it is a LoRA on Gemma 4 plus a linear
     position head.
   - Not to be run as third-party code.
