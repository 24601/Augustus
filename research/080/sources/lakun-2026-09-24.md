## What it is

A **non-generative** multimodal typed-decision scorer, about 0.756B parameters (their count; the
ModelScope inspector says 751,022,406, a 5.4M disagreement, and neither is verified here). Joint
full fine-tune of `jhu-clsp/mmBERT-base` plus the vision tower of
`google/siglip-so400m-patch14-384`, fused by resampling SigLIP patches to 64 visual tokens through
a `LayerNorm` + `Linear` bridge, with a vendored Laya decision head and a proper-scoring objective.
JEV is named as inspiration only: there is **no TypeSafe client, no Jev output, no hosted
typed-decision labels, no preference pairs and no reward model** anywhere in the source.

## Why it is not an arm, a comparator, or a mechanism to borrow

**The labels are a generalist's pseudo-labels, and the score is agreement with that same
generalist.** Their own words: "The labels were generated by `qwen3.8-flash`, not verified by
people", and the reported result is "agreement with the teacher's pseudo-labels was: Choice
86.14%, Score 85.21%, Noul 98.13%" on 12,703 held-out groups. Image rows carry
`label_source.kind = "vlm"`.

So the headline is **teacher agreement, not accuracy**, on a test set labeled by the same family
that labeled training. The author says this plainly — "These are **not** general-purpose accuracy,
reasoning, or probability-calibration scores" — which is more honest than most of the field, and it
is still the circularity trap M5's design lock names for T1, found in the wild. Text train and test
are both `deepseek_synthetic` over the same 50 topics, so the generator is shared too.

It also cannot be a comparator we score ourselves: the weights carry **no license**, and the data
provenance is unfinished by the author's own admission. Nothing is reproducible from the
checkout — no JSONL, no images, no base weights, no tokenizer, no checkpoint, no `report.json`, and
no labeling or generation code.

Two things it does **not** do wrong, worth recording because we check for them: checkpoint
selection is on validation loss with the test split read once, not on the reported split; and no
calibration is fitted on the reported split, because none is fitted at all. The CLI prints
"未经概率校准" — not probability-calibrated.

## The one thing worth taking

**Their measured position bias is a usable number for our order probe.** In the dataset profile,
among 4-way choice questions the teacher's answer is **B in 53.15% of image rows and 46.48% of
text rows**, while **D is 1.74% and 2.98%**. That is a generalist teacher placing nearly all its
answers in one or two slots, and it is exactly why the multimodal ladder's F5 requires an
option-reorder flip rate and why the Jev-Omni card demanded a permutation probe before trusting an
option-position head. LaKun shuffles option order during training and reports that the test
agreement does not state whether it was shuffled, which is the gap that makes the number
interpretable rather than decisive.

Their group-level split — groups, not individual questions — is the same rule as our same-split
families row in §3.4, independently arrived at.

## Disposition

**Discovery record only.** Not an arm, not a comparator, no mechanism imported. Do not import the
rows, the teacher labels or the checkpoint, and do not cite 86.14 / 85.21 / 98.13 as anything but
agreement with `qwen3.8-flash`. The durable fact for the record is narrow: a sub-1B mmBERT+SigLIP
typed head was trained against a generalist VLM and LLM teacher and then scored against that same
teacher.
