# decider — card (2026-09-25)

`Mapika/decider`, created 2026-09-16, 72 commits, 328 stars, **Apache-2.0 including the weights**.
Mark Marosi. An independent open reproduction of the System One class on Qwen3.5 bases: 0.8B, 2B,
4B, a 35B-A3B MoE, an NVFP4 build and a vision variant. Read from the README and, for the
contamination question, from the source of `decider/data/` via the librarian. Everything below is
**Reported** unless marked as read from source.

This is the most substantial artifact in this class the record contains. It ships the training
recipe and the data builders, not only weights; it serves `POST /v1/systemone` in TypeSafe's wire
format; and it runs on CPU and Apple Silicon as well as CUDA.

## The headline claim needs correcting

It has not topped JevBench. The repository's own standings table, read 2026-09-21, puts **Jev
1.13.0 first at 75.4** and SemIf second at 74.7, with **decider-35b-a3b at 68.9** of 36 entries and
decider-2b at 64.6. On the Decision Index, edition v0.1, decider-35b-a3b is **fourth of 32** at
54.3 behind Jev at 59.5 and two zero-training wrappers; the defensible claim it does make is that
it is the highest-scoring *trained* model on that edition.

The repository is careful about this and says both leaderboards were "read on the dates given; we
did not run them," which is better provenance hygiene than most. The overstatement is in the
retelling, not in the source.

## Read from source: it is trained on three of our four evaluation corpora

The decisive fact, from `decider/data/core.py` rather than the README:

| Corpus | In training? | Identifier and caps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BANKING77 | **yes** | `mteb/banking77`, train and test, capped 20,000 / 1,500 |
| CLINC150 + OOS | **yes** | `clinc/clinc_oos` config `plus`, train and test, same caps; `oos` relabelled |
| CivilComments | **yes** | `google/civil_comments`, `train[:400000]` capped at 12,000, `test[:60000]` |
| HotpotQA | **no** | no task, loader or dataset id anywhere under `decider/data/` |

The held-out mechanism is a per-task `heldout=True` flag that drops a whole dataset from the train
list. It is applied to 24 other tasks and **not** to these three. `banking77` is additionally
reused as a probe built from its eval split while its train split still enters the mixture, which
is not an exclusion.

So decider is the first named instance of the contamination gate recorded at
`sources/bev-decision-150k-2026-09-24.md` and wired into plan v4's M5 row. **T2a, T2b and T2c are
all inside its training data.** A decider score on any of them is not a held-out score, and no
seeded partition of ours changes that, because the leak is upstream of the partitioner.

E3's corpus is clean: HotpotQA is absent from the mixture entirely.

## What is genuinely good here, and useful to us

- **The compute-envelope claim gets third-party evidence.** Plan v4 §3.7 argues the trainer must
  work regardless of the agent's hardware. decider reports the 2B at about 4 GB, a CPU path in
  bfloat16 for both the library and the server, and an Apple Silicon MPS path with numbers rather
  than adjectives: held-out MASSIVE Scenario accuracy 0.7553 and ECE 0.0438 against the published
  bf16 row's 0.756 and 0.041. That is a typed-decision model of useful quality inside **E-cpu**.
- **Reproduction is claimed and measured.** One epoch is 1.47M examples and 455M tokens, 5.3 h on a
  GH200, and is reported to match v9 within noise on the 94-task set (in-task 0.809 against 0.812,
  held-out 0.739 against 0.741), with the fitted temperature moving 1.36 → 1.03. The README also
  says the released weights came from staged continuation runs and reproduction is **not**
  byte-identical, which is the right way to state it.
- **Question independence is a design property, not a hope.** Every question can be scored in its
  own row, so adding, removing or reordering questions cannot change another answer, and every
  Score level is judged alone without its number or its neighbours. That is the cleanest available
  answer to the aggregation trap our own guidance names.
- **The limits section is unusually honest** and several items are directly useful: one pass cannot
  do multi-step arithmetic; the 2B's top-label ECE on hard items is **0.30**, confident where it is
  wrong; rules written into the question are not followed at this size (0.67 for a one-sentence
  question against 0.24 for a paragraph of rules); picking a record out of a long JSON array by
  position is the worst input shape (0.51 against 0.70); the 27B teacher "agreed with only 72% of
  its own generic-option labels."

## Disposition

**Run it, and label the row.** The contamination is established from source rather than assumed:
T2a, T2b and T2c are all inside its training data, so a decider score on them is not a held-out
score and cannot carry a generalization claim. That is one sentence on one row. It is not a reason
to leave the cell empty, and an earlier version of this card treated it as one.

The weights are Apache-2.0, the recipe is public, and it runs on CPU and Apple Silicon, which makes
it one of the few things in this record we can actually run ourselves. Three uses, in order of what
they are worth:

1. **A declared-contaminated reference on T2a–c.** What an off-the-shelf open decision model scores
   on these exact rows, measured by us. Not evidence about the superpopulation; still the only
   answer to "is our whole ladder in the right range at all".
2. **An uncontaminated comparator on E3.** HotpotQA is absent from its mixture entirely, so that
   comparison carries a held-out claim. Needs its own frozen suite and our own run.
3. **A measured compute envelope for the trainer skill.** Its MPS and CPU numbers are exactly what
   §3.7 asks recipes to carry, and they are someone else's measurements, which is worth more than
   ours.

Its ECE of 0.30 on hard items at 2B is also a direct, dated instance of the hazard the vibecheck
card names: a probability served with an answer is not a calibrated probability, and the size at
which that breaks is measurable.

## Run notes, 2026-09-25: measured on M5, with our own envelope numbers

`Mapika/decider-4b` at revision `eb5fbdfc`, v2.1, on a Colab L4 (22.03 GiB), peak reserved
12.66–18.52 GiB. All 78,845 rows, one action per id, every string inside `options_enumerated`.
Declared contaminated: the three M5 corpora are in its training mixture.

### 161× between GPU and CPU on identical work

Same VM, same weights, same revision, same prompts, same 200-row prefix, only the device changed:
**41.84 rows/s on the L4 against 0.2592 rows/s on CPU**, 2.32 GiB resident against 8.24. That is
our own measurement of the envelope this card previously supplied only as the publisher's numbers,
and §3.7 now has a first-party figure for one artifact form.

It also answers the portability question honestly rather than optimistically. decider *runs* on
CPU, which is true and is what its card says. At 0.26 rows/s, T2c's 60,000 rows would take 64 hours.
**"Runs on CPU" and "is usable on CPU" are different claims**, and the second one needs a rate.

### torch.compile is a net loss at this scale

Its Engine defaults to `torch.compile`. End to end on T2b: **14.30 rows/s with compile against 41.84
without** — roughly six minutes of inductor work per (batch, length) bucket for about 1.4× steady
state, and an OOM at a 64-row batch. Every reported run keeps the CUDA graphs and skips the compile.
A default that costs a third of throughput on a 12,845-row job is worth knowing before a recipe
recommends it.

### Order sensitivity scales with the option set

| task | options | flip rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T2c | 2 | 0.33% |
| T2b | 2 | 5.33% |
| T2a | 77 | **10.67%** |

**On T2a about one row in nine is decided by option order rather than by the text.** T2b's 16 of 300
reproduced exactly across two independent sessions on two different L4 VMs, which is the
determinism check worth having beside a flip rate. Five arms have now been measured this way and
only AnyJev's L0 reaches zero, at two options, by paying cyclic shifts for it.

### One deviation, documented rather than quiet

decider received **the one-sentence decision plus the enumerated options, not the spec's guidance
paragraph**, on the reading of its own README that a paragraph of rules scores 0.24 against 0.67 for
one sentence at this size. Defensible, and recorded — and it still means this arm answered a
shorter instruction than every other arm in the experiment. Its row is labelled reduced-instruction
wherever it appears. The interesting consequence is that a decision model whose own documentation
says detailed instructions hurt it is a different kind of artifact from one that improves when the
spec is enumerated, which is what A2a did this morning.

### Operational

Five L4 runtimes were reclaimed 54–60 minutes after creation, every time, independent of task,
batch size or memory in use, across six sessions. Two things fixed by the run rather than worked
around: `decider-ai` pins numpy < 2 and a plain install downgrades Colab's numpy, breaking the
preinstalled torch 2.11 — `pip install --no-deps decider-ai` against numpy 2.1.3 runs correctly.
