# E1 receipt: cost-aware thresholds and prior shift, confirmed (2026-09-24)

Operator: an Amp thread on the runner `tabputer` (`/mnt/tst`, user `basit`), thread
`T-01a0d413-fff9-768a-b321-6ae4730772cc`. Fit as `augexp` on the GPU, scoring as `augctl` on the
host, which is the only principal holding confirmation labels.

**Result: the paper's central claim holds decisively, and one of its supporting claims is false.**
Cost-aware plug-in thresholding beats a stale 1:1 threshold at every powered held-out ratio, by up
to 4.8 points of cost. Intercept-only recalibration beats re-thresholding under prior shift, by 2.3
points from a 200-label budget. But **A is not non-inferior to retraining at extreme cost ratios**,
on CivilComments, at the pre-registered 2% margin. The convenient half of P9 did not survive.

## The controls, and where they failed

The controls are the point of E1 as much as the numbers are, and three of them fired on real
defects rather than on nothing.

- **The fit cannot write predictions.** A separate post-lock program does, and it holds no
  checkpoint: it re-derives every arm from the same partitions with the same seeds and refuses to
  write unless the recomputed temperature matches the lock's. It matched **exactly**, hours later
  in a fresh container, on both corpora, as did the shift intercept. A checkpoint would have had to
  be trusted; this was checked.
- **The scorer refuses without a matching recorded lock.** It had no recorded lock to match: M4
  published the splits before any analysis lock existed, so the manifest had no slot for one. The
  binding was added by `augctl` before any label was read, and `prereg/README.md` records that a
  post-publication binding is a custodian's assertion, with the public commit and the earlier,
  hashed prediction artifacts carrying the ordering instead.
- **The refusal arrived too late to be a refusal.** `main()` read the labels and only then called
  the guard. Fixed at [`0c4d2f9`](https://github.com/24601/Augustus/commit/0c4d2f9); the check now
  precedes opening the label file. Verified negatively, with an audit hook counting label opens:
  a wrong hash exits 2 with `label_open_attempts: 0`.
- **Power comes from the lock, not from the scored data.** The scorer originally recomputed it with
  the equivalence formula for every mode, which both understates superiority rows and lets the data
  decide which claims count.

## Result: what is refuted

**A is not non-inferior to retraining at extreme ratios.** The margin is 2% of A's cost, fixed in
the design lock.

| Corpus | Contrast | Δ | Interval | Margin | Reading |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- | ---: | --- |
| Civil | 4:1 A − B-retrain | −0.00023 | [−0.00031, −0.00014] | 0.00031 | **non-inferior** |
| Civil | 1:19 A − B-retrain | +0.00095 | [+0.00074, +0.00116] | 0.00052 | **fails** |
| Civil | 1:49 A − B-retrain | +0.00066 | [+0.00051, +0.00081] | 0.00029 | **fails** |
| Civil | S A-recal − B-retrain_S | +0.00244 | [+0.00218, +0.00269] | 0.00107 | **fails** |

Retraining is genuinely better where the cost ratio is extreme, by about 2–4% of A's cost. The
honest statement is narrower than the convenient one: *you can reach the right decision by moving
the threshold, and on this data you still pay a few percent for not retraining.* B-retrain_S is
additionally handed the exact target prior, so this is its strongest form, which makes the failure
more informative rather than less.

**Equivalence to the cost-conditioned arms does not hold.** Of the four powered equivalence rows
among Civil's nineteen primary contrasts, exactly one reads equivalent: 1:19 E − A, Δ = +0.00016,
[−0.00009, +0.00040] inside 0.00052. (The lock's count of eleven is over all 34 reported rows,
fifteen of which are the training ratios and descriptive.)
C and C\* diverge from A by up to 0.063 at 1:49 — an order of magnitude outside any margin. Feeding
the cost ratio in as a feature is not a substitute for thresholding on it.

**CLINC carries no equivalence or non-inferiority claim at all.** Its analysis lock said so before
anything was scored: at 12,845 confirmation rows every such contrast needs 0.3M to 10M rows. The
prespecified INCONCLUSIVE row applies, and the five unpowered CLINC rows that happen to read
favourably are not outcomes.

## A powered contrast that is still inconclusive

CLINC 4:1 A − B-stale is powered and reads nothing: Δ = −0.00174, [−0.00586, +0.00237], which
straddles zero. Power was computed against the design lock's planning effect of −0.042, taken from
the synthetic population. The realized effect at that ratio is a twenty-fifth of it. Power is a
statement about an assumed effect, and the assumption was wrong here; the interval, which is
distribution-free, is the thing that decides, and it declines to.

## Limits

- σ̂ came from the calibration split; the realized sd differs, and every table above reports the
  realized interval, not the planned one.
- Coverage needs independent units of the declared sampling unit; equal inclusion probability alone
  does not give that.
- The shifted population is a reweighting of the confirmation split to a higher prior, not new
  evidence, and its realized prior is recorded beside each S interval (Civil 0.243465 at
  n = 1,662,869; CLINC 0.169139 at n = 14,562).
- One corpus is CivilComments and one is CLINC150. Two datasets are two datasets.
- The recorded-lock binding was written after publication. That weakness is stated in
  `prereg/README.md` rather than smoothed.
