# E1 receipt: the NVIDIA replication (2026-09-26)

Operator: an Amp thread on the Mac, running its own Colab session `e1nv-01a0dc07`. The NVIDIA side
received inputs and emitted predictions; it held no confirmation label, computed no loss, and
scored nothing. `augctl` grades.

**Result: E1's conclusions are not hardware-dependent.** Every fit-split and calibration-split
quantity the two platforms share agrees to at least seven decimals, most of them bitwise. The
fitted temperature moves by 6.0 × 10⁻⁸ on both corpora. The three conclusions written down before
the run — cost-aware thresholding beats a stale 0.5 threshold, recalibration removes most of the
prior-shift penalty, and P9's non-inferiority half is refuted — all reproduce, on the split this
side can compute. Confirmation predictions for all 1,367,024 Civil and 12,845 CLINC rows are
handed over for scoring.

## The instrument reproduced, not substituted

CLINC, and every fit-split and calibration-split number on both corpora, came from
`e1_fit.main()` and `e1_predict.main()` called directly, with one substitution: `embed`, wrapped so
that it calls the original `e1_fit.embed` on 16,384-row contiguous slices and writes each slice to
disk the moment it exists.

Civil's 1.37M-row prediction file needed more than that, because `e1_predict.py` writes its output
only after every row is embedded, and three separate VMs died with a nearly finished forward pass
in memory. `exp/`-equivalent `e1_stream.py` produces the same file in 65,536-row chunks, each
committed to disk and copied off the VM as it lands. Its arms come from `e1_fit`'s own functions —
`build_arms` included — and its derivation sequence and arm expressions are byte-copies of
`e1_predict.py` lines 100–186, temperature guard included. Chunking is sound because every arm
written is a **rowwise** function of that row's embedding; `A_tuned`, the one arm whose value
depends on the whole split, is not written by `e1_predict.py` either.

**That claim is checked rather than argued.** Running `e1_stream.py` on CLINC and comparing to the
`predictions-clinc.json` that `e1_predict.main()` itself produced:

```
ids identical: True 12845
arm key sets identical: True 23
arms bitwise identical: True
differing bits per arm: {}
temperature equal: True   intercept equal: True   lock hash equal: True
```

23 arms × 12,845 rows, not one bit different. The streaming path is the same instrument.

The re-derivation control fired as designed, and harder than intended. `e1_predict.py` and
`e1_stream.py` recomputed the temperature from scratch in fresh processes on **four different L4
VMs** and matched the analysis lock built on the first one with **drift exactly 0** every time,
as did the shift intercept. A re-run of the whole Civil fit on the second VM produced a fit report
byte-identical to the first. Both prediction files are lock-bound and scorable.

## GPU verification by memory delta, not by a flag

An install log and `torch.cuda.is_available()` both lie. The driver program records
`nvidia-smi --query-compute-apps=pid,used_gpu_memory` immediately before and after the first
forward pass and matches on its own pid:

```
before: (no compute apps)                 this pid 5279, mine = null
after : "5279, 1146 MiB"                  this pid 5279, mine = 1146 MiB
```

A 1,146 MiB delta appearing under this process's own pid, from a GPU that reported 3 MiB total in
use beforehand. During the Civil prediction runs the same query attributed 1,600 MiB to pid 10407
and 1,454 MiB to pid 4184, the latter alongside `utilization.gpu` at 100% and `memory.used` at
1,462 MiB — the card's total being within 8 MiB of what our process alone was charged.
`torch.cuda.memory_reserved()` agreed at 920 MiB, which is the allocator's share of the same number
rather than independent evidence; the nvidia-smi per-pid attribution is the evidence.

## What failed

Stated plainly, because it shaped the run.

**Colab reclaimed four of five VMs mid-run**, at roughly 65, 55, 80 and 55 minutes of session life.
The costly one was the first: a finished 1,367,024-row Civil prediction existed on disk and the VM
was reclaimed before it was copied off. That was my error, not Colab's — packing and copying the
output was a step I took minutes after the run finished instead of inside it. The driver now packs
and hashes the prediction file as the last action of the run, and `e1_stream.py` commits and ships a
result every 65,536 rows.

The recovery is the measurement worth keeping: on the fourth reclaim, 14 of 21 chunks (917,504 rows)
were already on the Mac, and the fifth session re-uploaded them, re-derived the arms — drift 0
against the lock built two VMs earlier — and finished the remaining 449,520 rows. Total Civil GPU
time spent across all attempts was roughly 2.4 h for 37 min of necessary work. **A 40-minute
contiguous GPU job is not reliably schedulable in this envelope; a chunked one is.** §3.7's rule for
`E-ephemeral` was right, and the first attempt did not honour it.

Also failed, and worked around: `colab drivemount` returns `ValueError: mount failed` without
interactive auth, so there was no durable filesystem on the VM side; and `colab exec` hangs
indefinitely once its websocket drops, while `colab download` keeps working, so progress was polled
by downloading the log.

## Limits

- **Colab is not the pinned container the plan asked for.** `plan/nvidia-replication.md` routes E1
  and E3 to a rented box running our own image precisely so that the environment can be stated,
  and it says a replication whose environment cannot be attributed is weaker than none. This run
  was directed to Colab, so the environment is Google's choice on the day and it is *not* the same
  environment tabputer had: transformers 5.16.1 against tabputer's pinned version, torch
  2.11.0+cu128, CUDA 12.8, driver 580.82.07. More than the hardware changed. The honest reading is
  that this replicates E1 across *platform*, not across *card alone*, and that the agreement is
  therefore over a larger perturbation than the plan intended — which makes the agreement stronger
  evidence and a disagreement harder to attribute. None was found.
- The confirmation predictions are unscored here and unscorable here. Every number above is from
  the fit or calibration split.
- The comparison of contrast means and σ̂ is against the fit report on tabputer, not against a
  rerun on tabputer, so within-vendor rerun instability is not separated from cross-platform
  difference. E3's determinism receipt measured the former on one vendor.
- A_tuned is not written, for the reason `e1_predict.py` gives: tuning its threshold needs labels
  this principal does not have.
- Civil's prediction file came from `e1_stream.py`, not `e1_predict.py`. The CLINC bitwise check
  above is strong evidence that they are the same instrument, but it is a check on 12,845 rows of
  one corpus, not a proof.
- Two platforms are two platforms.

### The lock-hash distinction, which is the guard working

The NVIDIA predictions are bound to **their own** lock hashes, computed from the fit and calibration
run on that machine — not to the lock recorded with our split. The scorer was given the recorded
hashes, which is the check that runs before any label file opens; **passing the NVIDIA hash would
have made it refuse.** Margins therefore differ in the sixth digit, because each lock derives its
own margin from its own σ̂.

That is the design behaving correctly rather than a discrepancy to reconcile. A replication produces
a new instrument, a new lock and a new margin; scoring it against the recorded lock is what keeps
the comparison honest, and the guard exists precisely so nobody can score predictions against a lock
they were not bound to.

results reported. E1 and E3 replicate in full. The only thing that did not reproduce is E3's
