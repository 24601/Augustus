# M5 grading, and the three defects it found in its own harness

2026-09-25. Fifteen task-arm checkpoints and A2a graded as `augctl` against confirmation labels
that never left tabputer-1. Written while A2a and A2b are being re-run, because the defects below
are a result and waiting for the re-run would make them look like a footnote to it.

**Every defect found here punished the arms that read the spec and spared the arms that learn from
fit rows.** That is not a neutral bug. It sits directly across the comparison M5 exists to make —
artifact form against artifact form — and each one, uncorrected, would have shown a hand-written
program or a compiled program losing for reasons that have nothing to do with being a program.

## The custody split held

Colab compiled and predicted; it held no confirmation label and computed no loss. The actions came
back and `augctl` scored them on the host. The scorer, the specs and the label files were hashed
before and after each grading, the action files were copied off the exFAT mount to a directory
`augctl` owns before being read, and the hashes matched on both sides of every copy.

Three gradings exist and all three are kept: `m5-scores-checkpoints.json` (`605d6cd2…`),
`m5-scores-surface.json` (`e6c595a4…`) and `m5-scores-specs3.json` (`25bba648…`). None was
overwritten. They are not drafts — the sequence is the evidence that the numbers moved for stated
reasons, and a single final file would have destroyed it.

The re-exported bundle is `83c17239…`, 23,281,155 bytes, `m5-specs-3`. Its six fit and confirmation
digests are unchanged from the bundle before it, which is the check that the rows did not move and
only the instructions did.

## Defect 1: an arm graded against a vocabulary its spec never named

The T2c spec tells every arm the two options are `toxic` and `not toxic`. The stored confirmation
labels are the integers 0 and 1. The scorer built its label set from the stored values, so the
compiled program emitted exactly what it was told to emit and was graded **60,000 invalid outputs
out of 60,000**, mean cost 0.3090, accuracy 0.

The arms that learn from fit examples were untouched, because they copy whatever surface those
labels happen to use.

Fixed by declaring an answer surface once, in `m5_specs.ANSWER_SURFACE`, and rendering both sides
through it; a stored value and the spec's wording are accepted as two ways of writing one answer.
A2a T2c became mean cost **0.1295**, accuracy 0.5237, invalid 0. Every fit-based arm's score was
bit-identical before and after, which is the check that this is a relabelling and not a thumb on
the scale.

Polarity was taken from the rule that produced the label — `toxicity >= 0.5` is the positive class
at 0.07991 — and not from the minority count, which would have been the wrong way to settle it.

## Defect 2: a spec that described a task the labels do not encode

T2b's spec said the answer was one of 150 in-scope intents or `out of scope`. Its labels have only
ever been two values, because E1 built it as binary out-of-scope detection. An arm that read that
spec answered a question nobody was scoring, and no mapping repairs that.

Two collisions fell out of it. The scorer's abstain label for T2b was `out of scope`, which is one
of the two real answers, so an arm that correctly called a query out of scope was charged 0.3 for
abstaining rather than 0.0 for being right. And A1's T2b program was a router over label names,
which for labels named `0` and `1` yields no cues at all: every row fell through to the fallback
and it answered `out of scope` 12,845 times out of 12,845. Its 0.3000 above is the price of
abstaining on everything, not a measurement of a hand-written program. Under the corrected cost
table that same checkpoint reads 0.9419 with accuracy 0.0581, which is the stored-1 rate: an arm
that says out of scope on every row is right on exactly the out-of-scope rows. Both readings are
of a program written against a spec that misdescribed the task, and both are superseded by the
re-run.

Polarity again came from the construction rule — CLINC's label is `intent = OOS class 42`, positive
rate 0.05660 — with the confirmation partition's 746 of 12,845 at 0.0581 as corroboration rather
than evidence.

## Defect 3: options named by reference to an artifact that cannot look them up

The T2a spec said `one of the 77 BANKING77 intent labels`. A person resolves that; a compiled
program cannot. The compiler receives one spec string with a handful of worked examples inside a
5,120-token budget, so A2a was never shown the 77 names.

It invented **1,717 distinct intent-shaped strings** over 6,000 rows — `transfer_not_recognised`,
`refund_request`, `card_activation` — snake_case like the real labels and not among them. 4,291 of
6,000 unmatched, mean cost 0.8534, accuracy 0.0950.

This one is shared. The invention is a real property of the artifact form; being asked for a
vocabulary it was never given is a defect in the spec. The bundle now enumerates every allowed
answer verbatim, built from fit labels, with the worked examples rewritten into the same wording,
and A2a is being re-run against it. Both numbers will be reported. If the enumerated run still
invents names, the property is the arm's; if it does not, the first number measured the spec.

## What the matcher did not do

The invalid-output rule was fixed before any score was seen: exact match, then normalized, and
anything still unmatched costs the wrong-answer cost rather than counting as an abstention. It was
not touched after the scores appeared, and it should not be. Every repair above is upstream of it —
in the spec, the surface, or the cost table — which is where a defect of this kind belongs. A
matcher that had been widened to rescue `toxic` against a stored `1` would have hidden all three.

## What writing the options out actually bought

A2a re-run against bundle `83c17239…` under `m5-specs-3`, with every allowed answer enumerated in
the spec and the worked examples rewritten into the same wording. Programs hashed before any
confirmation input, as before.

| | distinct outputs, by-reference spec | distinct outputs, enumerated spec | options |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | 1,749 / 6,000 | **1,477 / 6,000** | 78 |
| T2b | 358 / 12,845 | **3 / 12,845** | 3 |
| T2c | 2 / 60,000, none of them a label | **2 / 60,000, both labels** | 2 |

**A small option set is fixed completely by writing it out, and a large one is not.** T2b and T2c
now emit exactly the declared surface and nothing else — `in scope` 11,188, `out of scope` 1,032,
`abstain` 625; `toxic` 39,644, `not toxic` 20,356. T2a still emits 1,477 different strings for a
78-option decision, which is only a 16% reduction.

What did change on T2a is the kind of string. The by-reference run invented intent-shaped names
that were not labels. The enumerated run's most frequent outputs are real BANKING77 names copied
from the list — `card_payment_fee_charged` 502, `abstain` 482, `failed_transfer` 350 — so the
program is selecting from the enumeration rather than inventing around it, and still cannot hold
to the set. Grading will say whether the remaining strings are variants of real names or something
else; that is what decides whether the 77-way row measures the artifact form or measures a
5,120-token prompt budget.

**The enumeration cost nothing.** T2a's rendered spec is 4,434 bytes and still carries all 24
worked examples alongside the 77-line option list; T2b 2,170 bytes and T2c 6,337 bytes, both also
24. The concern that writing out 77 labels would evict examples from the budget was wrong, and it
was worth measuring rather than assuming in either direction.

Compile and inference, L4: T2a 36.1 s and 20.39 rows/s, T2b 32.6 s and 26.10 rows/s, T2c 43.9 s
and 23.22 rows/s. Program sizes are within 7 KB of each other across the three tasks, which is
what a fixed-size compiled artifact looks like regardless of how many options its spec names.

A1 was re-run on all three tasks under the same specs. Its T2b program no longer abstains on
everything: `in scope` 12,264 and `out of scope` 581, against a true out-of-scope rate of 746.
R1, R2a, R2b and R3a were not re-run, and their scores stand from the third grading.

### P11 is refuted on one task and confirmed on another

Plan v4 predicted in advance that "programs lose on fuzzy real text". On T2c the hand-written
program costs 0.0587 and beats R1, R2a and R2b, losing only to R3a's 0.0479 — a lexicon of attack
cues with a quotation-and-negation discount, against three fitted heads over a frozen encoder, on
60,000 CivilComments rows. On T2a it costs 0.4058 against R2a's 0.1177 and the prediction holds
exactly as written.

The difference is not corpus difficulty; it is how much of the decision the label names carry.
T2c's decision is binary and its cue structure is writable by hand. T2a's is 77-way and its label
names are all a rule-writer has, which is why A1's T2a program abstains on 30% of rows.

### Enumerating the options helped the arm that was already close and not the one that was not

A2a's T2b went from a superseded reading to **0.1217 at accuracy 0.8442 with invalid 0**, emitting
only `in scope`, `out of scope` and `abstain`. Its T2c emits only `toxic` and `not toxic`.

Its T2a did not recover: **3,691 of 6,000 unmatched, invalid 0.6152**, against 0.7152 before. The
classification of those unmatched strings is the finding. Using the frozen normalizer as a
description rather than as a matcher — prefix either way, or one token-stripped form containing
the other — **414 unmatched rows are near-misses of a real label and 3,277 are not intent names at
all**. The arm selects 33 of the 77 declared names for the 2,309 rows it gets right, and invents
`refund_request`, `transfer_time`, `card_activation` for most of the rest. Near-misses like
`top_up_limit` against the real `top_up_limits`, and `verify_identity` against `verify_my_identity`,
are 82 distinct strings out of 1,440.

So writing the options out changed what the program reaches for and did not make it hold to the
set. A 5,120-token prompt budget is a fact about how this artifact was compiled, and this grading
cannot separate it from the artifact form; neither reading is asserted here.

### One thing this grading could not do

The A2a files were written by a hand-rolled driver — the runner ran one task at a time through
`compile_program` and `run_program` because `m5_colab` raises on A2b inside its loop — so their
actions are keyed by row id with no arm and no program record, and `main()` refused them. They were
scored by importing the frozen `score_arm` instead, with the same labels, costs and aliases. The
refusal was right: binding an arm name by guessing from a filename is how a mislabelled result
enters a table. `m5_colab` now takes `--rung` and `--task`, so running one rung alone no longer
needs a driver that writes a different file shape.
