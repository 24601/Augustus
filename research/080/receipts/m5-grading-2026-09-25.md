# M5 grading, and the three defects it found in its own harness

2026-09-25. Fifteen task-arm checkpoints and A2a graded as `augctl` against confirmation labels
that never left tabputer-1. Written while A2a and A2b are being re-run, because the defects below
are a result and waiting for the re-run would make them look like a footnote to it.

**Every defect found here punished the arms that read the spec and spared the arms that learn from
fit rows.** That is not a neutral bug. It sits directly across the comparison M5 exists to make —
artifact form against artifact form — and each one, uncorrected, would have shown a hand-written
program or a compiled program losing for reasons that have nothing to do with being a program.

## The custody split held

Colab compiled and predicted; it held no confirmation label and computed no loss. The actions came
back and `augctl` scored them on the host. The scorer, the specs and the label files were hashed
before and after each grading, the action files were copied off the exFAT mount to a directory
`augctl` owns before being read, and the hashes matched on both sides of every copy.

Three gradings exist and all three are kept: `m5-scores-checkpoints.json` (`605d6cd2…`),
`m5-scores-surface.json` (`e6c595a4…`) and `m5-scores-specs3.json` (`25bba648…`). None was
overwritten. They are not drafts — the sequence is the evidence that the numbers moved for stated
reasons, and a single final file would have destroyed it.

The re-exported bundle is `83c17239…`, 23,281,155 bytes, `m5-specs-3`. Its six fit and confirmation
digests are unchanged from the bundle before it, which is the check that the rows did not move and
only the instructions did.

## Defect 1: an arm graded against a vocabulary its spec never named

The T2c spec tells every arm the two options are `toxic` and `not toxic`. The stored confirmation
labels are the integers 0 and 1. The scorer built its label set from the stored values, so the
compiled program emitted exactly what it was told to emit and was graded **60,000 invalid outputs
out of 60,000**, mean cost 0.3090, accuracy 0.

The arms that learn from fit examples were untouched, because they copy whatever surface those
labels happen to use.

Fixed by declaring an answer surface once, in `m5_specs.ANSWER_SURFACE`, and rendering both sides
through it; a stored value and the spec's wording are accepted as two ways of writing one answer.
A2a T2c became mean cost **0.1295**, accuracy 0.5237, invalid 0. Every fit-based arm's score was
bit-identical before and after, which is the check that this is a relabelling and not a thumb on
the scale.

Polarity was taken from the rule that produced the label — `toxicity >= 0.5` is the positive class
at 0.07991 — and not from the minority count, which would have been the wrong way to settle it.

## Defect 2: a spec that described a task the labels do not encode

T2b's spec said the answer was one of 150 in-scope intents or `out of scope`. Its labels have only
ever been two values, because E1 built it as binary out-of-scope detection. An arm that read that
spec answered a question nobody was scoring, and no mapping repairs that.

Two collisions fell out of it. The scorer's abstain label for T2b was `out of scope`, which is one
of the two real answers, so an arm that correctly called a query out of scope was charged 0.3 for
abstaining rather than 0.0 for being right. And A1's T2b program was a router over label names,
which for labels named `0` and `1` yields no cues at all: every row fell through to the fallback
and it answered `out of scope` 12,845 times out of 12,845. Its 0.3000 above is the price of
abstaining on everything, not a measurement of a hand-written program. Under the corrected cost
table that same checkpoint reads 0.9419 with accuracy 0.0581, which is the stored-1 rate: an arm
that says out of scope on every row is right on exactly the out-of-scope rows. Both readings are
of a program written against a spec that misdescribed the task, and both are superseded by the
re-run.

Polarity again came from the construction rule — CLINC's label is `intent = OOS class 42`, positive
rate 0.05660 — with the confirmation partition's 746 of 12,845 at 0.0581 as corroboration rather
than evidence.

## Defect 3: options named by reference to an artifact that cannot look them up

The T2a spec said `one of the 77 BANKING77 intent labels`. A person resolves that; a compiled
program cannot. The compiler receives one spec string with a handful of worked examples inside a
5,120-token budget, so A2a was never shown the 77 names.

It invented **1,717 distinct intent-shaped strings** over 6,000 rows — `transfer_not_recognised`,
`refund_request`, `card_activation` — snake_case like the real labels and not among them. 4,291 of
6,000 unmatched, mean cost 0.8534, accuracy 0.0950.

This one is shared. The invention is a real property of the artifact form; being asked for a
vocabulary it was never given is a defect in the spec. The bundle now enumerates every allowed
answer verbatim, built from fit labels, with the worked examples rewritten into the same wording,
and A2a is being re-run against it. Both numbers will be reported. If the enumerated run still
invents names, the property is the arm's; if it does not, the first number measured the spec.

## What the matcher did not do

The invalid-output rule was fixed before any score was seen: exact match, then normalized, and
anything still unmatched costs the wrong-answer cost rather than counting as an abstention. It was
not touched after the scores appeared, and it should not be. Every repair above is upstream of it —
in the spec, the surface, or the cost table — which is where a defect of this kind belongs. A
matcher that had been widened to rescue `toxic` against a stored `1` would have hidden all three.

## What writing the options out actually bought

A2a re-run against bundle `83c17239…` under `m5-specs-3`, with every allowed answer enumerated in
the spec and the worked examples rewritten into the same wording. Programs hashed before any
confirmation input, as before.

| | distinct outputs, by-reference spec | distinct outputs, enumerated spec | options |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | 1,749 / 6,000 | **1,477 / 6,000** | 78 |
| T2b | 358 / 12,845 | **3 / 12,845** | 3 |
| T2c | 2 / 60,000, none of them a label | **2 / 60,000, both labels** | 2 |

**A small option set is fixed completely by writing it out, and a large one is not.** T2b and T2c
now emit exactly the declared surface and nothing else — `in scope` 11,188, `out of scope` 1,032,
`abstain` 625; `toxic` 39,644, `not toxic` 20,356. T2a still emits 1,477 different strings for a
78-option decision, which is only a 16% reduction.

What did change on T2a is the kind of string. The by-reference run invented intent-shaped names
that were not labels. The enumerated run's most frequent outputs are real BANKING77 names copied
from the list — `card_payment_fee_charged` 502, `abstain` 482, `failed_transfer` 350 — so the
program is selecting from the enumeration rather than inventing around it, and still cannot hold
to the set. Grading will say whether the remaining strings are variants of real names or something
else; that is what decides whether the 77-way row measures the artifact form or measures a
5,120-token prompt budget.

**The enumeration cost nothing.** T2a's rendered spec is 4,434 bytes and still carries all 24
worked examples alongside the 77-line option list; T2b 2,170 bytes and T2c 6,337 bytes, both also
24. The concern that writing out 77 labels would evict examples from the budget was wrong, and it
was worth measuring rather than assuming in either direction.

Compile and inference, L4: T2a 36.1 s and 20.39 rows/s, T2b 32.6 s and 26.10 rows/s, T2c 43.9 s
and 23.22 rows/s. Program sizes are within 7 KB of each other across the three tasks, which is
what a fixed-size compiled artifact looks like regardless of how many options its spec names.

A1 was re-run on all three tasks under the same specs. Its T2b program no longer abstains on
everything: `in scope` 12,264 and `out of scope` 581, against a true out-of-scope rate of 746.
R1, R2a, R2b and R3a were not re-run, and their scores stand from the third grading.

## Fourth grading: the two spec-reading arms, re-run and re-scored

`m5-scores-a1-v3.json` (`c16209aa…`) and `m5-a2a-v3-imported-score.json` (`0b747295…`), both under
`m5-specs-3`, both with invalid rate 0 on T2b and T2c. The R arms were not re-run and their scores
stand from the third grading. Four reports now exist and none was overwritten.

| arm | T2a | T2b | T2c |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | 0.4058 | **0.0986** | **0.0587** |
| A2a | 0.8404 | 0.1217 | 0.1578 |
| R2a | **0.1177** | 0.0607 | 0.0691 |
| R3a | 0.1488 | **0.0323** | **0.0479** |

Mean cost. R1 and R2b omitted for width; they sit between R2a and R3a on every task.

### P11 is refuted on one task and confirmed on another

Plan v4 predicted in advance that "programs lose on fuzzy real text". On T2c the hand-written
program costs 0.0587 and beats R1, R2a and R2b, losing only to R3a's 0.0479 — a lexicon of attack
cues with a quotation-and-negation discount, against three fitted heads over a frozen encoder, on
60,000 CivilComments rows. On T2a it costs 0.4058 against R2a's 0.1177 and the prediction holds
exactly as written.

The difference is not corpus difficulty; it is how much of the decision the label names carry.
T2c's decision is binary and its cue structure is writable by hand. T2a's is 77-way and its label
names are all a rule-writer has, which is why A1's T2a program abstains on 30% of rows.

### Enumerating the options helped the arm that was already close and not the one that was not

A2a's T2b went from a superseded reading to **0.1217 at accuracy 0.8442 with invalid 0**, emitting
only `in scope`, `out of scope` and `abstain`. Its T2c emits only `toxic` and `not toxic`.

Its T2a did not recover: **3,691 of 6,000 unmatched, invalid 0.6152**, against 0.7152 before. The
classification of those unmatched strings is the finding. Using the frozen normalizer as a
description rather than as a matcher — prefix either way, or one token-stripped form containing
the other — **414 unmatched rows are near-misses of a real label and 3,277 are not intent names at
all**. The arm selects 33 of the 77 declared names for the 2,309 rows it gets right, and invents
`refund_request`, `transfer_time`, `card_activation` for most of the rest. Near-misses like
`top_up_limit` against the real `top_up_limits`, and `verify_identity` against `verify_my_identity`,
are 82 distinct strings out of 1,440.

So writing the options out changed what the program reaches for and did not make it hold to the
set. A 5,120-token prompt budget is a fact about how this artifact was compiled, and this grading
cannot separate it from the artifact form; neither reading is asserted here.

### One thing this grading could not do

The A2a files were written by a hand-rolled driver — the runner ran one task at a time through
`compile_program` and `run_program` because `m5_colab` raises on A2b inside its loop — so their
actions are keyed by row id with no arm and no program record, and `main()` refused them. They were
scored by importing the frozen `score_arm` instead, with the same labels, costs and aliases. The
refusal was right: binding an arm name by guessing from a filename is how a mislabelled result
enters a table. `m5_colab` now takes `--rung` and `--task`, so running one rung alone no longer
needs a driver that writes a different file shape.

## R4-gliner2, a rung added after the ladder was built

A GLiNER2.5-base fine-tune, run with `uv-scripts/classification`'s `train-gliner2.py` unmodified,
five epochs on a stratified 1,600 of each task's 2,000 fit rows, 654 s of training for all three
tasks and about 11.7 compute units. It trains only on fit examples already in the bundle, so it is
an R-rung rather than an external model. Confirmation actions: 77, 2 and 2 distinct strings, every
one a member of `options_enumerated`.

### The free option is worse than a constant, except where the label space is wide

Its own held-out-fit numbers, reported because the recipe requires reporting them, and not to be
confused with the confirmation grading:

| task | options | zero-shot | fine-tuned | majority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a intent | 77 | 0.6948 | 0.8610 | 0.0223 |
| T2b scope | 2 | 0.6450 | 0.9575 | 0.9450 |
| T2c toxicity | 2 | 0.3575 | 0.9250 | 0.9175 |

**On a 77-way decision, reading the label names gets 0.695 with no training at all — 31× the
majority floor — and 7.8 minutes of fine-tuning buys +0.166. On both binary decisions the
zero-shot model is far below a constant answer**, 0.645 against 0.945 and 0.358 against 0.918, and
the fine-tune beats the constant by 1.25 and 0.75 points, inside the noise band the recipe's own
notes warn about at 400 eval rows. Only macro-F1 (0.73 and 0.75 against a ~0.48 floor) shows the
binary models doing anything at all.

This is the same shape as the Hub task tagger's 0.102 zero-shot under a 0.320 constant, now
measured twice on different tasks. A label-reading encoder is worth its fit where there are many
labels to read and worth very little where there are two.

### Option order moves a label-reading model, and fitting it stops that

First 300 confirmation rows of each task, run again with the label list reversed:

| task | fine-tuned | zero-shot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | 0.0267 | 0.1100 |
| T2b | 0.0000 | 0.2033 |
| T2c | 0.0000 | 0.0167 |

**One T2b answer in five changes when two option strings are swapped**, before fitting. After
fitting, none of 300 changes on either binary task. So a zero-shot number from a model that reads
its options is not a stable quantity in the way a fitted one is, and a rung that reports only
zero-shot accuracy is reporting something that moves when the spec is reordered. AnyJev publishes
the same class of measurement — 0.230 for raw logits against 0.073 for its zero-label L0 — and
until today no arm of ours reported it at all.

### Two policy facts to read its cost against

It never abstains. No fit example carries the abstain option, so the arm emits plain argmax, which
is what keeps it comparable with R2a and R3a and is also a choice that a 0.3-versus-1.0 cost matrix
punishes. And on T2b it emits the costly rare class on 2.3% of confirmation rows against 5.5% in the
fit set — under half its base rate — while T2c's 8.4% toxic matches its 8.3%. Under T2c's 4:1
false-negative cost, argmax is knowingly not the cost-minimal rule either.

### Graded, and its T2c advantage does not survive its own interval

| task | R4-gliner2 | best prior | R4 training |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | 0.1655 | R2a 0.1177 | 469 s |
| T2b | 0.0454 | R3a 0.0323 | 87 s |
| T2c | **0.0453** | R3a 0.0479 | 98 s |

Invalid 0 and abstention 0 on all three, 77 / 2 / 2 distinct outputs. **The lowest T2c cost in the
experiment comes from 98 seconds of fine-tuning, against the 4,500 seconds R3a spent on the same
task** — forty-six times the training for a slightly worse result. On T2a it loses to R2a's head
over a frozen encoder, and on T2b to R3a.

Its no-abstain policy did not cost it against A1 on T2a — 0.1655 against 0.4058, where A1 abstains
on 30% of rows. And its rare-class under-emission on T2b is real but not disqualifying: emitting
`out of scope` on 2.3% of rows against a 5.8% true rate costs it the rows it misses, yet 0.0454 is
still below the 0.0581 that always answering `in scope` would cost, so the arm is doing work rather
than impersonating a constant.

**These intervals are exploratory, and that is not a formality.** The registered family is six arms
with `K_FAMILY = 6`, fixed before any score was seen. R4-gliner2 was added after every other arm
had been graded, so its multiplicity is not the registered multiplicity and no non-inferiority or
superiority verdict from it carries the pre-registered guarantee. The same will apply to imajev,
AnyJev L0 and decider. The honest form is an exploratory row labelled as one, not a
pre-registered-looking row that is not, and `K_FAMILY` is not being edited to a number chosen after
seeing results.

### The paired contrast, which takes the T2c win back

R4 and R3a graded in one invocation so the differences are paired over shared rows. R3a's scores
reproduce the specs-3 report exactly and R4's reproduce its own. Difference is R4 minus R3a, so
negative favours R4.

| task | mean difference | interval | reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | +0.0167 | [−0.0045, +0.0379] | R4 costs more, interval spans zero |
| T2b | +0.0131 | [+0.0060, +0.0202] | R4 costs more, interval clear of zero |
| T2c | −0.0025 | [−0.0052, +0.000079] | R4 cheaper on the point estimate, **interval includes zero** |

**So "R4 beats R3a on T2c" is not a result, and the heading above this section originally said it
was.** Two report means of 0.0453 and 0.0479 look like a win; the paired interval on 60,000 rows
puts the upper bound at +0.000079, a hair the wrong side of zero. The honest statement is that R4
is at worst indistinguishable from R3a on T2c and at best cheaper by 0.0025 on a scale whose
wrong-answer costs are 0.25 and 1.0 — while costing 98 seconds of training against 4,500. **The
training-cost claim survives; the accuracy claim does not.**

On T2b the difference goes the other way and its interval is clear of zero: R3a is genuinely
cheaper there, by about 0.013.

The scorer set `non_inferior` true on T2c at k=6. **That flag is not being reported as a finding.**
It is a mechanical reading of an interval whose multiplicity is wrong: R4 entered after every other
arm had been graded, and imajev, AnyJev L0 and decider are not in the registered six either. The
interval is the evidence; the boolean beside it was computed under a family size that no longer
describes what was run.

## AnyJev L0 graded: order-invariance without labels, and a decision worse than a constant

Exploratory, outside the registered six, no contrast computed. Invalid 0 and abstention 0 on all
three; `none of these` won **0 of 6,000** T2a rows, so the partitioning never fell through to its
escape option.

| task | AnyJev L0 | R4-gliner2 | best fitted arm | always-majority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a (partitioned) | 0.3723 | 0.1655 | R2a 0.1177 | — |
| T2b | **0.3259** | 0.0454 | R3a 0.0323 | **0.0581** |
| T2c | 0.1015 | 0.0453 | R3a 0.0479 | — |

**On T2b the zero-label arm costs 0.3259 against the 0.0581 of answering `in scope` every time.**
It is five and a half times worse than a constant. The reason is visible in its outputs: it emits
`out of scope` on 3,818 of 12,845 rows against a true rate of 746. An unlabelled readout has no way
to learn that the rare class is rare, because the base rate is exactly the thing labels carry. Its
T2c split is the same story — 25,490 `toxic` against a true 8.4%.

So the two properties come apart cleanly. **L0 buys exact order-invariance for zero labels and does
not buy a usable decision on a skewed class.** It beats A1 (0.4058) and A2a (0.8404) on T2a and
loses to every fitted arm on every task. Against R4-gliner2, whose entire fit was 87 seconds on
1,600 labelled rows, it is seven times the cost on T2b.

That pair of facts is the useful one for a recipe. Order sensitivity is a defect you can fix with
no labels at all; calibration to a base rate is not, and a few hundred labels fix it. AnyJev's own
levels say as much — L1 exists and takes 100–500 labels — and this is that claim measured on an
outside task rather than argued.

The T2a row stays marked **partitioned**: 77 options split into four fixed sub-questions because the
readout caps at 26, global argmax across them. It is not the same question shape as any other arm's
T2a and is not comparable to R2a's 0.1177 without that note.

## imajev graded: contamination looks exactly like you would expect, on two tasks of three

Declared contaminated, exploratory, no contrast, no generalization claim. Invalid outputs on T2c
are exactly the 19 literal `unknown` strings, as predicted before grading.

| task | imajev-4b | best non-contaminated | note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T2a | **0.1163** | R2a 0.1177 | trained on BANKING77 |
| T2b | **0.4062** | R3a 0.0323 | trained on CLINC, and still worst but one |
| T2c | **0.0418** | R4 0.0453 | trained on CivilComments |

**It posts the lowest cost in the experiment on two of the three tasks it was trained on, which is
what a contaminated row is supposed to look like and is why it carries a label rather than a
ranking.** The useful part is not that it wins; it is that the margin is thin — 0.1163 against
R2a's 0.1177 on T2a, and 0.0418 against R4-gliner2's 0.0453 on T2c, where R4's entire fit was 98
seconds on 1,600 labelled rows. A model that has seen the corpus beats a two-minute fine-tune by
0.0035.

**And on T2b it loses catastrophically, at 0.4062 against a majority constant's 0.0581.** It calls
out of scope on 38.2% of rows against a 5.8% base rate. Training on CLINC did not save it, because
what M5 asks is not what CLINC's own task asks: the stored labels are binary in-scope detection,
and a model trained on 151-way intent routing brings a prior that does not transfer. Contamination
helps where the decision matches the one the corpus was built for, and not otherwise.

### A scorer bug the grader found, and the fix

The report printed `abstention_rate` 0.0003167 for T2c — the same 19 rows. The cause:

```python
if matched == costs.get("abstain_label"):   # None == None on a task with no abstain label
```

On T2c there is no abstain label, so the lookup returns `None`; an unmatched output is also `None`;
and the comparison counted **every invalid output as an abstention** — precisely the rule this file
was written to prevent, inverted by one comparison. It is the same bug that printed
`abstention_rate` 1.0 for A2a on T2c in the first grading, flagged then as meaningless and
root-caused now.

Fixed with an explicit `matched is not None` guard. **No cost changes**: `cost_of` never consulted
that counter, so every `mean_cost` in every report stands. Only the abstention counter on
abstain-less tasks was wrong, and `invalid_rate` was correct throughout — which is why the grader
reporting `invalid_rate` as the real number, twice, was the right call both times.
